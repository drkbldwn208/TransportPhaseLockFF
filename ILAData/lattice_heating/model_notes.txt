LATTICE HEATING FROM THE FAST I/Q CAPTURES — 14 OCTOBER 2026

Purpose and scope
-----------------
Estimate initial single-particle motional excitation of a 741 nm optical
lattice from the measured laser frequency noise. The reference laser is
assumed noiseless. The numerical tables are conditional predictions, not
measurements of atomic heating and not predictions of trap lifetime.

Inputs: laser_iq_FPGA_shortest_0.npz through _9.npz, 8x tracking gain,
FRA off, 15.36 MS/s, 17.07 ms each. Records are analyzed independently.
The source hashes and numerical outputs are in heating_summary.json.

Assumed isotope: 162Dy. Er/h = 2242.98 Hz; Er/kB = 107.65 nK.
Assumed initial state: lowest Bloch band, q=0; one lattice axis.
Mean depth is calibrated independently. The isolated 741 nm optical line
dominates the dispersive potential. Fixed optical intensity is assumed.
Detuning Delta = laser frequency minus atomic resonance, in Hz.
The main figures use negative (red) detuning. Blue results are also saved.

There was no supplied scan range or atom-to-mirror distance when this
calculation was made. The map covers |Delta|=2--50 MHz and depth=1--150 Er;
the deeper extension locates the main servo-noise resonances. Geometric
shaking is quoted per square metre of atom-to-retro-mirror distance.

From I/Q to a fluctuating potential
----------------------------------
Unwrap phase at the original sampling rate FIRST. Adjacent phase differences
give frequency in Hz: delta_nu = fs*diff(phi)/(2*pi). Equivalently,
S_nu(f) = f^2 S_phi(f), for phi in radians and one-sided PSDs per Hz.

Filter the derived frequency at 1 MHz offline, trim edge transients, and
remove its small record mean. This limits high-frequency detector noise in
the nonlinear conversion; it does not modify the FPGA or experimental loop.
At the principal motional transition frequencies, this filter is flat.

For weak fluctuations, epsilon = delta_depth/mean_depth = -delta_nu/Delta,
and S_epsilon = S_nu/Delta^2.

For the nonlinear calculation, form
    depth(t) = Delta / [Delta + delta_nu(t)]
    epsilon(t) = depth(t)/mean(depth) - 1.
This keeps the mean calibrated depth fixed. The quadratic and higher terms
can mix noise at high frequencies down to a small band gap. It is a
quasistatic dispersive model, not a calculation of photon scattering.

Welch PSDs use 32768 samples, 50% overlap, and a Hann window. PSD bin spacing
is 468.75 Hz. A Gaussian of sigma=one bin gives 1.10 kHz FWHM spectral
smoothing. Figures show mean PSDs; capture-to-capture spread is recorded,
not interpreted as a formal confidence interval. Narrow danger-zone
boundaries are consequently approximate at the ~kHz gap-frequency scale.

Replacement for the harmonic Gehm/Savard approximation
----------------------------------------------------
Use H/Er = (p/hbar/k)^2 + s*sin(k*x)^2, in plane waves
exp[i*(2*n+q)*k*x], with q in [-1,1]. Diagonalize this Hamiltonian and
evaluate the actual band gaps and matrix elements.

For noise PSD S_epsilon in 1/Hz, the transition rate from band 0 is

 R(n<-0) = 2*pi^2*(s*Er/h)^2 * |<n|sin(k*x)^2|0>|^2 * S_epsilon(f_n0).

 Initial ground-band depletion = sum_n R(n<-0).
 Initial energy deposition    = sum_n h*f_n0*R(n<-0).

These are distinct observables. In the harmonic limit, Gehm/Savard give
Gamma_E = pi^2*f_trap^2*S_epsilon(2*f_trap). For the harmonic ground state,
R(0->2)=Gamma_E/4, and each excitation adds 2*h*f_trap. The code checks this
factor and checks convergence against a larger plane-wave basis.

At q=0, depth noise excites even bands. Higher bands can matter strongly:
at 10 Er, the 0->2, 0->4 and 0->6 gaps are approximately 17.4, 41.2 and
85.8 kHz. Their matrix elements differ greatly; overlap alone is insufficient.
A uniform quasimomentum average (appropriate to a localized Wannier state)
is also evaluated. It changes the predicted 5 and 10 Er rates by about
8% and 15%, respectively, for this spectrum. Other initial populations,
including excited bands, need separate weighting.

are saved in heating_summary.json.

Depth danger zones
------------------
At -10 MHz and q=0, the main 0->2 transition crosses 65 kHz near 65.4 Er
and 86 kHz near 108.1 Er. The measured broad peaks produce maxima near
65.5 Er (~8 /s) and 109.5 Er (~31 /s). Rates exceed 5 /s around 62.5--71.5 Er,
and exceed 10 /s around 105.5--121.5 Er. These are conditional on the measured
noise and the assumed isotope/depth calibration, not universal boundaries.

Earlier weaker crossings occur at 30.6 Er (0->4, 65 kHz) and 45 Er
(0->4, 86 kHz). Near 10.3 Er the 0->6 gap reaches 86 kHz, but its small
matrix element keeps the total rate small. Further features occur near
125 Er (0->4, 175 kHz) and 143 Er (0->2, 100 kHz).

Detuning scans
--------------
At fixed calibrated depth, linear noise heating scales as Delta^-2.
Nonlinear down-conversion can break this scaling at small detuning:
at 5 Er the red-detuned rates at 10, 5 and 2 MHz are approximately
0.00073, 0.0055 and 0.17 /s; at 10 Er they are 0.0053, 0.0266 and 0.44 /s.
Treat the 2--5 MHz region as provisional, not a safe operating prediction.

At fixed optical power, depth also changes with detuning. This moves the
band gaps across noise peaks, so increasing detuning need not give a
monotonic improvement. Use measured depth and the map together.

Close to resonance, optical spectral tails can cause direct scattering;
an instantaneous inverse-detuning model does not capture this. At the
deepest/shallowest-detuning combinations, weak optical excitation also
becomes a poorer approximation. Zeeman or hyperfine components require
their own detunings and dipole matrix elements.

Separate geometric shaking term
-------------------------------
For atom-to-mirror optical distance L, tau=2*L/c. The incident and returning
fields sample the same laser phase at different times. At f*tau << 1,
    delta_x = L*delta_nu/nu_optical,
    S_x = (L/nu_optical)^2*S_nu.
The full-band perturbation uses sin(2*k*x) and excites odd bands at q=0.
At 5 and 10 Er, the additional depletion rates are respectively
8.4e-5 and 8.4e-4 /s * (L/1 metre)^2. This term does not fall with detuning
when the lattice depth is held fixed. Mechanical mirror motion is excluded.

Photon recoil and other omitted contributions
---------------------------------------------
For an isolated optical line with Gamma/(2*pi)=2 kHz, the antinode
scattering scale is
    Gamma_max = 2*pi*(2000 Hz)*(s*Er/h)/|Delta|.
At |Delta|=10 MHz this gives 14--28 /s at 5--10 Er. The simple standing-wave
model gives total mechanical recoil heating of order Er*Gamma_max, or
14--28 Er/s (1.5--3.0 microkelvin/s in energy/kB). This is a separate
single-line scale, not a full multilevel Dy or thermalization calculation.
Blue-node suppression of local scattering cannot simply be applied to
total recoil heating; momentum diffusion of a standing wave matters.

Unmeasured resonant optical tails can add scattering. The I/Q Nyquist limit
is 7.68 MHz, so these records do not bound optical power at an atomic
resonance 10 MHz away. Measurement noise also must not be mistaken for
optical spectral power. Many-body heating through fluctuating tunneling
and interactions, classical intensity noise, other lattice axes, envelope
forces, rare long-term drift/slips, and subsequent collisions are excluded.

Experimental consistency checks
------------------------------
1. Prepare the ground band, vary hold time, then band-map. At short times
   excited fraction should grow as R_out*t. Measure total number separately:
   high-band atoms can escape before thermalizing, leaving little change in
   the temperature of the retained cloud.
2. Calibrate gaps with a weak optical-frequency modulation scan. At fixed
   pulse duration and weak excitation, response scales as modulation
   amplitude squared; a coherent pulse initially gives probability ~t^2,
   not a stochastic constant rate. Use the actual modulation measured in I/Q,
   not the commanded DAC amplitude, because the feedback loop filters it.
3. Scan lattice depth at fixed detuning through predicted noise resonances.
   Band gaps and heating peaks should track the map; the primary allowed
   depth-noise transition at 5/10 Er is 11.7/17.4 kHz, respectively.
4. At fixed depth, double detuning and remeasure RF noise. Technical depth
   heating should fall roughly fourfold (with nonlinear corrections), while
   the isolated-line recoil scale falls about twofold. Geometric shaking
   does not change at fixed depth.
5. Compare loop settings at identical depth/detuning. Changes should follow
   the weighted PSD at band gaps, not total phase RMS or carrier linewidth.
6. Compare fluorescence/recoil, band populations, cloud energy and atom loss
   as different observables. Density dependence can identify collisional or
   many-body contributions that this single-particle model omits.
