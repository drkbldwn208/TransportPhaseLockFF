ADF4108 versus FPGA short I/Q captures, 2026-10-14
================================================

Run: python ILAData/compare_pll_fpga_iq.py
Outputs: pll_fpga_iq_comparison.json, pll_fpga_iq_spectra.npz, and three PNG/PDF pairs.

Conditions
----------
PLL: laser_iq_PLL_shortest_0...9.npz, physical beat 1000 MHz, FPGA reference
966.08 MHz. User confirms FRA off; analog settings believed unchanged.
FPGA: previous laser_iq_FPGA_shortest_0...9.npz, beat/reference 800 MHz,
8x FPGA tracking gain, FRA off. The captures are not simultaneous.
Both: 262144 samples at 15.36 MS/s, 17.0667 ms each, monitor decimation D=4.
The measured mean I/Q magnitude is approximately 11276 ADC counts for PLL
versus 1153 for FPGA. Normalizing fractional amplitude removes the scale,
but these are not a controlled same-carrier, same-input-power detector swap.

Mixer image: confirmed measurement artifact
------------------------------------------
Real ADC sample rate = 1966.08 MHz. A physical 1000 MHz tone is in the
second Nyquist zone and folds to 966.08 MHz. The reference setting is correct.
Its measured phase has the opposite sign to physical beat phase; the analysis
reverses this sign for cross-spectral phase comparisons.

The unwanted mixing term is at 1966.08 - 2*966.08 = +33.92 MHz. The existing
32-tap low-latency FIR passes it with relative amplitude 0.964095. The monitor
16-point mean at 245.76 MS/s attenuates it to 0.0905582, then decimation aliases
it to +3.2 MHz. Total predicted image amplitude relative to the desired carrier:
0.0873067. First-order spurious fractional AM RMS: 0.0617352 (6.17%).

Directly fitting the separated image to the complex conjugate of the desired
carrier gives amplitude ratios 0.08713...0.08730 in quiet PLL records. This
agreement identifies the feature as a DDC image, not independent optical AM.
Observed original broadband fractional AM is 6.58% RMS for resolved PLL
captures, versus 2.41% for FPGA. That comparison is dominated by the image.

Processing
----------
Both datasets receive the same eighth-order Butterworth complex low-pass,
2 MHz cutoff, forward/backward (offline, zero phase). No new decimation.
Trim 4096 samples at each end, then extract amplitude and unwrap phase at
15.36 MS/s. Do not take slow boxcar-averaged I/Q and unwrap it: that earlier
procedure produced false winding counts. The present filter preserves the
desired complex envelope while rejecting the spectrally separated image.

Varying the offline cutoff from 1.5 to 2.3 MHz changes the reported phase RMS
by <0.04% and fractional AM RMS by <5% in the three bands. These are processing
sensitivity checks, not an absolute optical calibration or detector-floor test.

Phase = physical beat phase relative to its reference. Fractional amplitude
a = abs(I+jQ)/mean(abs(I+jQ)) - 1. This is RF beat-voltage AM, not optical RIN.
Welch: Hann, 65536 samples, 50% overlap, 234.375 Hz bins. Six segments remain
per trimmed record. Only a segment mean is removed; no linear phase drift
subtraction. Records are analyzed separately and their spectra averaged.
Check: manual FFT normalization and CSD agree with SciPy welch/csd.

Cross PSD C = mean(conj(FFT(a))*FFT(phi)). Average complex cross spectra before
taking their magnitude. Magnitude-squared coherence = |C|^2/(S_aa*S_phi_phi).
It is unchanged by phase-to-frequency differentiation or a phase sign reversal.
It measures linear association, not the direction or physical source of coupling.

Quality and subsets
-------------------
PLL record 1 has deep I/Q minima and adjacent phase steps close to pi. Its
unwrapped excursion cannot be trusted and it is excluded from phase/CSD means.
This does not establish an optical-intensity dropout: off-frequency motion and
the measurement transfer function can also cause the recorded I/Q to collapse.
The other nine PLL records have strong magnitude and resolved phase steps.

PLL records 2,4,5,6,7 show persistent phase winding, roughly 1,7,43,5,1 turns
between endpoint averages. This is relative beat/reference phase; the data
alone cannot identify which laser/reference caused it. Records 0,3,8,9 form a
quiet subset: the 5 kHz low-pass phase spans less than one turn, with 1 ms edge
guards. All ten FPGA records meet that criterion. The full nine-record PLL
results are retained alongside the conditional four-record comparison.

Band results after image rejection
----------------------------------
Band          FPGA phase RMS   PLL quiet phase RMS   FPGA AM RMS   PLL quiet AM RMS
1-10 kHz      0.07665 rad      0.34517 rad           0.1258%       0.1018%
10-100 kHz    1.14732 rad      0.72359 rad           0.2758%       0.2520%
100k-1 MHz    0.69836 rad      0.70484 rad           0.8309%       0.7933%

Thus FPGA has 13.1 dB less integrated phase-noise power at 1-10 kHz than the
quiet PLL subset, but 4.0 dB more at 10-100 kHz; 100k-1 MHz is essentially equal.
Including all nine resolved PLL records raises its 1-10 kHz phase RMS to
0.96623 rad (22.0 dB more integrated power than FPGA). No overall linewidth
or causal detector advantage is inferred from these nonidentical conditions.

AM-phase correlation
--------------------
For a band, define phase-weighted coherence
F = integral(|C|^2/S_aa) / integral(S_phi_phi).
This has finite-sample positive bias and is NOT a measured causal AM->PM fraction.

Band          FPGA F   PLL (9) F   PLL quiet (4) F
1-10 kHz      0.1602   0.0932      0.1683
10-100 kHz    0.1914   0.0253      0.0509
100k-1 MHz    0.1249   0.0594      0.0803

Mismatch complete amplitude and phase records (300 random derangements) as a
control for chance correlation. The 95th-percentile mismatched values are:
Band          FPGA    PLL (9)    PLL quiet (4)
1-10 kHz      0.0453   0.0372     0.1580
10-100 kHz    0.0210   0.0225     0.0519
100k-1 MHz    0.0213   0.0207     0.0439

FPGA correlation around 65/86/175 kHz survives this control. PLL has some
correlation, particularly outside 10-100 kHz, but no demonstrated larger
broadband normalized correlation. Low-frequency quiet-PLL statistics are
limited by only four independent records and common spectral lines. Do not
interpret bootstrap-resampled coherence as an unbiased confidence interval;
the stored bootstrap intervals apply only to RMS phase and amplitude.

Absolute cross PSD can be larger for PLL at low frequency simply because its
phase noise is much larger. That does not by itself imply stronger AM sensitivity.
Possible shared causes include laser dynamics, actuator-induced AM, reference
noise, photodetection/RF conversion, and imperfect I/Q filtering. Coherence
cannot distinguish AM->PM from PM->AM or a shared disturbance inside feedback.

Controlled next measurement
--------------------------
For causal AM->PM sensitivity, apply small known AM to the detector-only RF
branch and observe the induced optical beat phase with an independent I/Q
monitor. Measure several frequencies; calibrate any phase modulation generated
by the AM modulator itself. A same-frequency, same-RF-power A/B detector swap
would make the present passive-noise comparison much more discriminating.
